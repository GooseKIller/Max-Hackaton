# How we talk to a 16-year-old

Design note. Status: proposed. This is a constraint on everything else we build.

**Review, 25 September:** treat the proposed tone as a testable editorial choice.
The literature does not establish which wording wins for our Kazan users. Prefer
[reviewed copy](takes.md) and [validation protocol](../research/validation.md) for
presentation claims; behavioural outcomes below are hypotheses, not measured results.

## The take

> Trying to be on the same wavelength as young people feels *worse* to young people
> than not trying at all.

This is correct, it is well documented, and it is the single largest risk to the design
in [emotional-anchors.md](emotional-anchors.md) — because the bridge copy and the mood
selector are exactly where this failure happens.

---

## 1. Why it backfires — three mechanisms stacked

### Persuasion Knowledge Model

People build up a mental model of how they are being persuaded. Once a persuasion
attempt is *recognised as such*, it gets discounted — and the model's central claim is
that **the receiver's perception matters more than the message's objective design.**
A well-crafted message that reads as a tactic performs worse than a plain one.

### Psychological reactance

People have an innate drive for autonomy and push back when they sense it is
threatened. Critically: **awareness of persuasive intent itself arouses reactance.**
So the harder the pitch, the stronger the resistance — independent of how good the
underlying offer is.

### Cringe is literally pain

Cringe is **vicarious embarrassment** — the response to witnessing someone's social
blunder or threat to their social standing. The literature describes it as **vicarious
social pain**. When an institution misuses slang, the teenager is not mildly unimpressed.
They are experiencing something the brain processes as social pain, and they associate
it with us.

### And now the part that makes this sharper than the take assumed

Persuasion knowledge is **developmental**. Research on 11–14 year olds shows that
knowledge of advertisers' tactics increases over that period, **together with distrust
of advertising claims.**

The proposed pilot includes 15–18-year-olds. The cited regional 55% figure does not
establish a national audience distribution or uniquely high sensitivity to persuasion.

## 2. The numbers

From SKIM's qualitative study of 543 people aged **18–25** across eight countries
(none in Russia); these are brand perceptions, not our pilot's copy-test results:

| Finding | Share |
|---|---|
| Value transparency above all else | **44%** |
| Reject brands for "trying too hard" with forced messaging | **~33%** |
| Earlier “20% unfollowed” claim | withdrawn: not supported by the linked SKIM report |

The most-cited specific tell, repeatedly, is **overuse of slang.**

A Russian-language study of slang in advertising reaches the same place: institutional
slang use "выглядит неуместным в глазах молодежи", and fast-obsolescing lexemes read as
superfluous and out of place. Slang has a shelf life measured in months; by the time it
reaches an institutional product it is already dead, and using a dead term is a stronger
signal of distance than using no slang at all.

## 3. Our specific exposure

We have three compounding risk factors:

1. **We are adjacent to a state program.** The Pushkin Card is government. For a
   teenager it is associated with school — with being taken somewhere as a group, on a
   Tuesday morning, by a teacher. This association needs interviews. The 22% share
   belongs to the synthetic catalogue and is not a verified real-Kazan measurement.

2. **Our best feature is a persuasion attempt.** The bridge — "ты смотрела «Войну и
   мир», вот тебе «Женитьба»" — *is* a recommendation with intent. Per the Persuasion
   Knowledge Model, if it is perceived as a tactic it gets discounted.

3. **Reminders may feel pressuring.** Test wording and frequency. A calm factual
   deadline with explicit opt-in can be useful; resistance is not a guaranteed outcome.

## 4. What we do about it

### Rule 1 — no slang. None.

Not one word. No «кринж», «база», «вайб», «го», «топчик», «краш». The register is
**neutral and direct** — the way a competent older friend talks, not the way an adult
imitates a teenager.

This costs us nothing. Slang is not what makes copy feel close; **precision** is.

### Rule 2 — be a tool, not a peer

We do not claim to be one of them. We claim to be useful. A tool that does its job well
is respected at any age. A brand pretending to be a friend is the exact thing the
research says gets unfollowed.

Concretely: no first-person chumminess, no emoji-as-personality, no exclamation marks
doing emotional labour.

### Rule 3 — expose the mechanism instead of hiding it

This one is the real insight, and it inverts the problem.

The Persuasion Knowledge Model says persuasion fails when it is *detected*. The usual
response is to hide it better. That is a losing race against a 16-year-old with a fresh
detector.

So do the opposite: **state plainly why we are showing something.**

> «Показываем, потому что ты отметил „Войну и мир“ и свободен в субботу вечером.»

An accurate explanation makes the recommendation inspectable. It does not guarantee
trust or make persuasion incapable of manipulation. Reasons must reflect real inputs.

And it costs nothing, because **the brief already requires it**: separate facts,
calculations and recommendations so the user understands where a result came from. The
compliance requirement and the anti-cringe strategy are the same action.

### Rule 4 — let the event be interesting, not the copy

An industrial tour of a power plant is genuinely interesting. It does not need
enthusiasm bolted on. Describe it flatly and it sells itself; describe it with
"это просто огонь" and we have made it worse.

The copy's job is to be **accurate and specific**. "Пустят в машинный зал" beats any
adjective.

### Rule 5 — preserve autonomy, especially in reminders

Reactance is triggered by imperatives. So:

- ❌ «Не упусти! Деньги сгорят 31 декабря!»
- ✅ «Последний указанный остаток — 3 200 ₽, от [дата]. Он ещё актуален?
  До конца года [N] дней.»

State the fact, offer options, let them decide. No countdown animations, no urgency
theatre. The fact is urgent enough on its own, and delivering it flatly is what makes
it credible.

This is also a positioning advantage. Our product's core promise — **choose for
yourself** — is structurally the opposite of the school-group excursion. Granting
autonomy is not just good tone, it is the product's actual differentiator.

### Rule 6 — the mood selector needs rewriting

In [emotional-anchors.md](emotional-anchors.md) I proposed a mood row reading
`поплакать · поржать · чтоб мозг взорвался · красиво · не один · убить два часа`.

Half of that is the trap. «Чтоб мозг взорвался» is an adult's idea of how a teenager
talks. Revised:

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| поржать | что-нибудь смешное |
| чтоб мозг взорвался | что-то необычное |
| не один | пойти с кем-то |
| убить два часа | недолго, рядом |
| поплакать | что-то сильное |
| красиво | красиво *(this one was fine)* |

Plainer, shorter, and it says the same thing without performing.

## 5. Who should write the copy

Not us, in the final version. Five people aged 15–18 reading our strings and marking
what makes them wince costs one afternoon and is worth more than any amount of internal
debate. We are not the audience and we cannot self-assess this.

If the team includes someone in the band, they own the copy — and per the brief's
team-ownership criterion, they should be the one who defends it to the jury.

## 6. How to test it cheaply

Fold into the survey we already planned. Show three versions of the same
recommendation — neutral, slightly warm, and deliberately youth-imitating — and ask
which one they would trust, and which makes them wince.

The prediction from the research is that neutral wins and youth-imitating loses badly.
If that holds, it is a defensible slide: we did not guess the register, we tested it.

## 7. Consequences for other docs

- [emotional-anchors.md](emotional-anchors.md) — mood labels revised per Rule 6; the
  bridge copy must follow Rules 3 and 4.
- [ranking.md](ranking.md) — the explanation layer (Stage D) is now a **trust**
  mechanism, not a nice-to-have. It is what keeps our persuasion from reading as
  manipulation.
- Reminder notifications — rewrite to Rule 5 before building.
